# Example scripts

One or more finished scripts for each shape in script.md. Use them to hear the voice and see the shape; never reuse their rules, names or lines. Shapes B–E were written to the pace and sentence length in the script.md table.

# Shape A: the rule is the rescue tool
Each runs about 75–85 seconds at 260 words a minute.

## 1. In This Town, Every Laugh Blows a BALLOON

In this town, every time you laugh, a balloon pops out of your mouth.
A giggle makes one the size of a grape. A real laugh makes one big enough to lift you off the floor.
Last year, our teacher laughed at her own joke and spent math class on the ceiling.
So nobody here laughs. The cinema only shows documentaries. Every classroom has a net.
And me? I'm the most serious kid in school. I have not laughed in 3 years.
My little sister Pia laughs at everything. Spoons. Pigeons. The word "pudding."
I pull her down by the ankle about 6 times a day.
Then, on Saturday, a pigeon stole a man's hat.
Pia laughed so hard she went up past the roofs and got stuck on top of the clock tower.
"Our ladder is 60 feet," the fire chief said. "She's at 200."
Then I got an idea. A balloon big enough could carry me up there.
But I'd have to laugh.
So I tried. "Ha." A grape. It lifted my shoelace.
I thought about pudding. A balloon the size of an apple. I went up 1 foot.
I tickled myself. A melon. 3 feet. Then I came back down.
I was out of ideas. Except one. The thing I never think about.
3 years ago, I laughed in the school play. I floated off the stage. In a carrot costume. Over the whole town.
I had been angry about it ever since.
I closed my eyes and pictured it. A carrot. Flying over the bakery. Waving.
And it was really, really funny.
Poof. A balloon the size of a bus.
I shot up past the roofs and grabbed Pia off the tower.
But I couldn't stop laughing. We kept going up.
"Stop!" Pia yelled. "Think of something boring!"
"Homework," I said. Nothing. "Dentist." Nothing.
Then the whole town looked up and saw us.
And 400 people laughed at once.
The sky filled with balloons. The mayor. The fire chief. My teacher, again.
We all came down slowly, around dinner time.
Grandpa came down on Tuesday.

## 2. In This Town, Saying Thank You Drops a COIN

In this town, every time you say thank you, a coin falls out of the sky.
A small thank you drops a penny. A big one drops something heavier.
A man once thanked a doctor for saving his life, and a gold coin went straight through his car.
So nobody here says it. People just nod. The mayor wears a helmet to birthday parties.
And me? I'm the rudest kid in school. I have never said thank you. My head has never been hit.
My grandma raised me. She runs the bakery. She gets up at 4 every morning. She saves me the corner piece.
I just nod.
Then, on Friday, a man in a suit taped a paper to our door.
900 dollars by noon, or the bakery closes. Grandma had 12.
Then I got an idea. A big enough thank you could pay for everything.
So I stood in the street and said it. "Thanks for the socks, Grandma."
A penny bounced off my head.
"Thanks for the corner pieces." A nickel.
"Thanks for getting up at 4." A dime. 16 cents.
I was out of small ones. Except one. The one I had never said to anybody.
I closed my eyes. "Thank you for keeping me. I never said it, because I was scared you'd think about it and change your mind."
The sky went dark.
A gold coin the size of a truck wheel came down. Right through the bakery roof.
The man in the suit took the coin and left very fast.
But now the roof had a hole the size of a truck wheel. That costs 900 dollars to fix.
Grandma looked at the hole. Then she looked at me.
"Thank you for staying," she said.
The second coin took the other half of the roof.
We sell bread outside now. It's actually better for business.
The man in the suit wears a helmet.

## 3. In This Town, "I'm Fine" Makes It RAIN

In this town, every time you say "I'm fine" and you're not, a rain cloud appears over your head.
A small lie makes a drizzle. A big one makes a storm.
My uncle said "I'm fine" at his own wedding, and they cut the cake in a boat.
So people here tell the truth, or they carry an umbrella. Our school has drains in every hallway.
And me? I'm the driest kid in town. I always say what's wrong. Loudly. Teachers hate it.
My best friend Juno is the opposite. She says "I'm fine" about everything. Her shoes squeak all year. She keeps a towel in her desk.
Then, in August, the hill behind the school caught fire.
The fire trucks ran out of water in 10 minutes. The river was dry.
Then I got an idea. A big enough "I'm fine" could put it out.
But it had to be a lie. And I don't have any.
I tried. "I'm fine with broccoli." A cloud the size of a plate. 3 drops.
"I'm fine with my haircut." The size of a table. It put out 1 bush.
"I'm fine that I lost the spelling bee." The size of a car. The fire didn't even notice.
I was out of small ones. Except one. The thing I had not said out loud all summer.
Juno is moving away. Tomorrow. 600 miles.
I looked at the fire and shouted, "Juno is leaving tomorrow, and I'm fine!"
Thunder.
A cloud the size of the whole town rolled in over my head. The fire was out in 1 minute.
But it didn't stop. It rained for 2 hours. The street turned into a river.
Juno walked up to me with water up to her knees.
"I'm fine too," she said.
The second cloud was bigger.
The moving truck floated away at 6 o'clock. They found it 2 towns over.
So Juno stayed one more week.
She gave me her towel.

# Shape B: the greedy one gets caught by the rule's own words
About 100 seconds at 250 words a minute. The wording ("every word") is said twice early and is the trap.

## 4. In This Town, the Mayor's Every Word Is a LAW

In this town, whoever holds the gold key at noon is mayor for a day.
And every word the mayor says becomes a law.
Every word. It pops up on the big sign in the square. Ding.
So mayors here talk very, very carefully.
Old Mayor Finch says about 9 words a day. He points at the rest.
Last year a mayor sneezed and said "bless me." We all had to. For a week.
My name is Wren, and I'm the reason our town had 46 laws before lunch.
It started on Friday. Mayor Finch put the key on a bench to tie his shoe.
One shoe. That's all it took.
I grabbed it at 11:59.
The clock struck 12. The key got warm. The sign blinked. "Hello, Mayor Wren."
The whole square turned and stared at me.
"Wren," Mayor Finch said. "Think before you…"
"Ice cream is free," I said. Ding.
The ice cream man dropped his scoop.
"School is closed." Ding. 200 kids cheered.
"My brother has to call me Queen." Ding.
"Yes, Queen," my brother said. He looked sick.
I climbed on the fountain. This was the best day of my life.
"Everybody walks backward!" Ding. The baker backed into a pond.
"Dogs get the sidewalk!" Ding. "Broccoli is illegal!" Ding.
By 12:30 I had 46 laws and a crown made of forks.
Mayor Finch walked up to me. Backward. It took a while.
"Wren," he said. "A mayor's day ends at midnight. Then the laws go away."
Midnight? I had so many ideas left.
So I said it. "I'm mayor forever. And nobody can ever take this key."
Ding.
Mayor Finch smiled. I did not like that smile.
"Wren," he said. "Every word. Did you hear yours?"
I looked at the sign. Forever. Nobody. Ever.
I tried to put the key down. It stuck to my hand.
"Somebody take it," I said. Nobody could. That was the law.
Then a lady handed me a paper. "The mayor signs these," she said.
"How many?" I asked.
"About 300 a day."
Mayor Finch went fishing. He's been fishing for 2 weeks.
He talks all the time now. He will not stop.
I've signed 4,112 papers so far.
Forever is a lot.
And my brother still has to call me Queen. That part's fine.

# Shape C: the town is afraid for the wrong reason
About 105 seconds at 215 words a minute. Very short sentences. Eight habits first, one shot each.

## 5. In This Town, Nobody Turns Off the LIGHT

In this town, nobody has turned off a light in 80 years.
Every lamp is on. At noon.
People sleep in sunglasses.
The dog wears a headlamp.
The cinema keeps its lights on. Nobody has seen an ending.
Birthday candles never get blown out. Grandpa's cake has 71. It's a fire hazard.
Kids play hide and seek. Nobody has ever hidden.
They say the Dark lives under the town.
One light goes out, and it comes up.
We moved in on Monday. Nobody told us.
At bedtime I turned off my lamp.
Nothing happened.
But a man across the street screamed.
A woman dropped 12 light bulbs.
A boy pointed at my window and ran.
At school they called me Switch.
Nobody sat near me. My desk had its own lamp. Two, actually.
But something was off.
My dark closet felt warm.
At night I heard knocking. From under the floor.
And every time I turned off a light, something far below went "ahhh."
On Thursday, the power plant broke.
Click. Every light in town. Gone.
Oh no.
The ground shook. The knocking got louder.
The street cracked open. A giant paw came out.
Everyone ran to the square with candles.
The mayor stood up front. He wore a hat with 9 lamps.
He owned the only bulb shop. He was very rich.
"You woke the Dark," he said. "You go talk to it."
They pushed me to the crack. And took 4 steps back. Big ones.
A head came up. It was the size of a bus.
It was a bear. In a nightcap.
It had bags under its eyes. Big ones.
It wasn't growling. It was yawning.
Then I understood the knocking.
It wasn't a monster. It was a neighbor.
80 years. Lights on. All night.
"I just," the bear said, "want. To. Sleep."
It never came up to eat anyone. It came up to complain.
I blew out my candle.
The bear sighed. The whole town shook, gently.
"Blow them out," I said. "He's just tired."
One by one, the candles went out.
And for the first time, the town saw stars.
Kids found out hiding works.
Grandpa blew out 71 candles. It took 4 tries.
And the mayor? The bear picked him.
Someone has to read the bedtime story. And nobody is more boring.
Chapter 3 tonight. 211 to go.

# Shape D: the newcomer breaks it (dark twist)
About 42 seconds at 225 words a minute. A storyteller, long flowing sentences, no speech. Nobody is shown hurt.

## 6. In This Town, Nobody Ever Looks UP

In this town, every single person walks around staring at their own shoes, wearing giant hats and carrying umbrellas on perfectly sunny days.
A new girl named Mina and her family moved in on a Tuesday and were handed 3 hats each before they even reached the front door.
Her parents decided it was some kind of local fashion thing and went along with it, while Mina became obsessed with finding out what was up there.
So one afternoon she climbed the tallest hill in town, threw her hat on the grass, and looked straight up at the sky.
Instantly, the big white cloud above the town opened one enormous yellow eye.
Every umbrella on every street snapped open at once as neighbors ran for their doors.
As it turns out, the town was never shy.
They had been living for years underneath a giant bird that feeds anything that looks up at it.
And Mina had just ordered worms for 3,000 people.

# Shape E: the cliffhanger
About 30 seconds at 170 words a minute. Slow, short, cut off mid-event.

## 7. The Town Where Every Kid Gets One KEY

In this town, every kid gets one key on their 10th birthday.
It opens exactly 1 door. Nobody tells you which.
Most kids find a shed. Or a closet.
But a girl named Juno got a key with no teeth.
It opened every door she tried.
The last one was a small red door behind the school.
Behind it was the same town. And the same girl. Holding the same key.
The other Juno whispered, "Don't let it close."
But the door was already moving.
